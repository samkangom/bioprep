Part 1. Product

1.1 What BioPrep is

BioPrep helps Indian students preparing for NEET (UG) practise Biology. A student takes a timed mock test that is scored like the real exam (+4 for a correct answer, -1 for a wrong one, 0 for a skipped one). Afterwards the student opens a solution. Instead of showing the answer straight away, a tutor asks two or three short guiding questions so the student reasons it out first. Only when the student has finished, or the 2:00 timer ends, or the student asks, do they see the answer, the written explanation and a video.

The business model is simple: a free plan gives 2 full explanations in total. After that, Rs 100 per test unlocks every solution and video in that test, forever.

Part 2. Features as user stories

Each story follows: As a [person], I want [action], so that [benefit]. Acceptance criteria are written so a tester can check each line. "Builders" notes say where the work lives and what is easy to get wrong.

2.1 Account and onboarding

AUTH-1

Sign in with my phone number

As a student, I want to sign in with my mobile number and a one-time code, so that I do not need to remember a password.

Acceptance criteria
  • The phone field accepts a 10-digit Indian mobile number starting 6 to 9; anything else shows a clear error.
  • A 6-digit code is sent by SMS. A wrong code shows an error and does not sign me in.
  • I can resend the code after 30 seconds; the button shows the countdown.
  • After signing in, reloading the page keeps me signed in. The sign-out button signs me out.
  • A returning user goes straight to the home screen; a new user goes to AUTH-2.
Builders: Supabase phone OTP (src/backend/remote.js: sendOtp, verifyOtp). SMS delivery needs a provider configured in Supabase; for India that means DLT-registered templates. Offline demo mode accepts any 4 digits.
AUTH-2

Introduce myself the first time

As a new student, I want to give my name, exam year and date of birth, and accept the privacy policy, so that my account is set up properly.

Acceptance criteria
  • I enter my name, choose NEET year 2027, 2028 or 2029, and enter my date of birth.
  • I must tick that I have read the privacy policy (it opens in a new tab and keeps my form).
  • Only my birth year is stored, never the full date of birth.
  • The form cannot be submitted without all of the above.
Builders: Server function complete_signup computes age from the date it receives and discards the date. Do not add a column for the full date.
AUTH-3

Parent or guardian consent when I am under 18

As a student under 18, I want my parent or guardian to agree before I start, so that the service is used with their knowledge.

Acceptance criteria
  • If my age is 13 to 17, a Parent or guardian consent section appears: relationship (mother, father, legal guardian), their full name, their 10-digit mobile number, and a consent tick.
  • If I am under 13, sign-up is refused with a clear message.
  • I cannot continue until every consent field is complete.
  • If I am 18 or older, none of this appears.
  • The consent record (name, number, relationship, policy version, time) is saved and deleted if my account is deleted.
Builders: The guardian is NOT verified (no OTP to their phone). It is a recorded declaration. Whether this meets the DPDP Act needs legal review. Minimum age 13 is MIN_AGE in src/privacy.js and in the SQL; change both together.
AUTH-4

Choose language and light or dark

As a student, I want to switch between English and Hindi and between light and dark themes, so that the app is comfortable to read.

Acceptance criteria
  • A language selector in the header offers English and Hindi. Meiteilon is listed as soon and cannot be selected yet.
  • My choice is remembered on this device.
  • The sign-in, header, footer and home screens change language; other screens are still English (see 4.2).
  • The dark mode button works and the app follows my phone's setting by default.
Builders: src/i18n holds English (source of truth), Hindi (first draft, needs native review) and an empty Meiteilon file. New student-facing strings go through t(key) and are added to en.js first.

2.2 Tests

TEST-1

See the tests I can take

As a student, I want to see Biology tests with their size, duration and my last score, so that I can choose what to practise.

Acceptance criteria
  • The home screen shows Biology as live, with the number of Botany and Zoology questions, and Physics and Chemistry as Coming soon.
  • Each published test shows its type, number of questions, duration in minutes, my last score if I have one, and either an unlocked badge or its unlock price.
  • Buttons: Start test or Retake test, View result (if I have attempted it), Unlock (if not unlocked).
  • A banner states how many free explanations I have left, or that they are used up.
TEST-2

Read the instructions before I start

As a student, I want to see the rules of the test first, so that nothing surprises me.

Acceptance criteria
  • The page shows question count, duration and marking (+4 / -1).
  • It explains the timer, mark for review, and auto-submit.
  • Start test begins the attempt.
TEST-3

Take a timed test

As a student, I want to answer questions one at a time with a timer and a question palette, so that it feels like the real exam.

Acceptance criteria
  • One question is shown at a time with four options. Option labels follow the question's chosen style (A B C D, 1 2 3 4, i ii iii iv, and so on).
  • A question may include a diagram.
  • Selecting an option highlights it; Clear response removes it; Save and next, Previous and the palette move between questions.
  • Mark for review flags a question. Palette colours and shapes show answered, not answered, marked, current and not visited, with a legend.
  • A countdown timer is always visible and changes style when 10 minutes and 2 minutes remain (colour plus the timer itself).
  • Time spent on each question is recorded.
Builders: The attempt is created on the server when the test page opens (start_attempt) so the server clock starts with the student's. Answers live only in the browser until submission. A page refresh mid-test loses the answers (see 4.2).
TEST-4

Submit my test

As a student, I want to submit by hand or have it submit itself at 00:00, so that my score is recorded fairly.

Acceptance criteria
  • Pressing Submit shows a summary (answered, not answered, marked, time left) and asks me to confirm.
  • At 00:00 the test submits itself.
  • If the network fails, my answers stay on screen with a Try again button; nothing is lost.
  • The score is calculated on the server, not in the browser. The browser never receives the correct answers.
  • A submission that arrives up to 5 minutes after the time limit is accepted (slow networks); later ones are refused with a clear message.
Builders: SQL function submit_attempt does the scoring and is idempotent: submitting twice returns the same result.

2.3 Results

RES-1

See how I did

As a student, I want my score, accuracy, number attempted and time used, so that I know where I stand.

Acceptance criteria
  • Four tiles: score out of the maximum, accuracy on attempted questions, attempted out of total, and time used.
RES-2

See where I am strong and weak

As a student, I want breakdowns by outcome, subject, chapter and time, so that I know what to revise.

Acceptance criteria
  • A donut shows correct, incorrect and skipped, with numbers and icons, not colour alone.
  • Bars show results for Botany versus Zoology and by chapter.
  • A time-per-question strip highlights questions that took more than 1.5 times the average.
RES-3

Review each question

As a student, I want a table of every question with my answer and whether it was right, so that I can go back to the ones I missed.

Acceptance criteria
  • Each row shows the question, my answer, and Correct, Incorrect or Skipped with an icon and text.
  • The correct answer is not shown in the table until I have opened that question's explanation; the cell says it is shown with the explanation.
  • Each row has an Explain button. Retake and Back to tests are available.
Builders: Deliberate decision: showing the answer here would bypass the tutor. Students still see which of their picks were right because that is the score.

2.4 Solutions: the Socratic tutor

SOL-1

Open a solution

As a student, I want to open the explanation for a question from my result, so that I learn from it.

Acceptance criteria
  • The server checks my entitlement first (see PAY-1 to PAY-4). If allowed, the tutor opens; if not, I see the locked card (PAY-2).
  • I can only open explanations for questions that were in a test I have submitted.
  • Prev and Next move between the questions of the test.
Builders: One gate decides this: SQL private.open_explanation, called only by the API (POST /api/explain/open). Staff opening a question for preview never use up a free slot.
SOL-2

Be guided to the answer

As a student, I want a tutor that asks me two or three short questions, so that I work out the idea myself.

Acceptance criteria
  • The tutor greets me and asks the first guiding question. A stage tracker (Recall, Reason, Apply) shows progress.
  • I can tap one of the answer buttons or type my own answer (up to 500 characters).
  • After each answer a chip shows On track, Partly there, Let us rethink or Hint given (icon plus text), and a You have worked out list builds up.
  • The tutor never states the correct option before the session ends. If its reply would give the answer away, the server replaces it with the built-in guide's reply.
  • After at most 5 of my messages the session ends.
Builders: The tutor is Claude when an API key is configured; otherwise, or if Claude fails or the daily limit is reached, a scripted keyword guide answers. The screen shows which one is helping. Prompts are built on the server from the database; the browser sends only {questionId, messages, kind, hintLevel}.
SOL-3

Ask for a hint or say I am not sure

As a student, I want up to 3 hints, each stronger than the last, so that I get unstuck without being told the answer.

Acceptance criteria
  • Give me a hint gives hint 1 (rephrase), then 2 (a concrete clue), then 3 (nearly gives it away). The button shows 1/3, 2/3, 3/3 and then stops.
  • I am not sure gives an easier version of the question and does not use a hint.
SOL-4

Have a fair time limit

As a student, I want a 2:00 timer per question, so that I do not stay stuck forever.

Acceptance criteria
  • A 2:00 timer counts down. It pauses while the tutor is thinking.
  • At 00:00 the explanation opens by itself.
  • The timer is also kept on the server: the browser cannot reveal the answer early by claiming time is up.
Builders: Server tracks started_at and paused_ms (time waiting on the model). A timeout reveal earlier than 117 seconds (120 minus a 3 second grace) is refused.
SOL-5

See the full explanation when I am done

As a student, I want the correct answer, a written explanation, why each wrong option is wrong, and a short summary, so that I understand and remember it.

Acceptance criteria
  • The explanation opens when the guided questions end, the timer ends, or I tap Show me the answer.
  • It shows: Answer with its label, the explanation, why each other option is wrong, Key idea, Common mistake, and the NCERT reference.
  • On the question card the correct option is now marked Correct with an icon, and my wrong pick is marked Your answer.
  • If I took part, a short Your thinking note shows what I did well, what to revisit and one exam tip.
Builders: Served by GET /api/explain/:questionId, which returns 403 until the server has marked the session finished. Nothing else carries these fields.
SOL-6

Watch the video explanation

As a student, I want a short video for each question, so that I can see the concept explained.

Acceptance criteria
  • The video panel is locked (with a message) until the tutor session ends.
  • Paid students can tap Skip to video to watch before the session ends.
  • Controls: play and pause, speed 1x, 1.5x, 2x, captions text, full screen.
  • If a question has no video yet, the panel says so.
  • If the video link expires during playback, the player gets a fresh link and carries on.
Builders: Videos are in a private bucket. POST /api/video-url signs a 10-minute URL only for an allowed session. Never expose the storage path to students.
SOL-7

Come back to an explanation I already earned

As a student, I want to reopen a finished explanation without redoing it, so that I can revise quickly.

Acceptance criteria
  • Reopening a question I already finished shows the explanation directly, with no new tutor chat.
  • It does not use another free explanation.
SOL-8

Save questions to revise

As a student, I want a Save to revision list button after an explanation, so that I can come back to hard questions.

Acceptance criteria
  • The button appears after the explanation opens, toggles saved or not, and the Profile screen lists saved questions.

2.5 Free plan, paywall and payments

PAY-1

Use the free plan

As a student, I want 2 free full explanations, so that I can try the tutor before paying.

Acceptance criteria
  • The header and solution screens show Free explanations left: N.
  • Opening an explanation for a question uses one free slot. Reopening the same question does not use another.
  • The two free slots are shared across all tests, not per test.
PAY-2

See clearly when I need to pay

As a student, I want a clear locked screen after my free explanations are used, so that I know what to do next.

Acceptance criteria
  • The locked card says my free explanations are used, shows my result for this question (right, wrong or skipped) and an Unlock for Rs 100 button.
  • The question and my own answer stay visible.
PAY-3

Pay Rs 100 to unlock a test

As a student, I want to pay with UPI or card, so that I get every solution and video in that test.

Acceptance criteria
  • Pay opens Razorpay's payment window (UPI and cards).
  • After I pay, the screen says Confirming your payment. It changes to Payment successful only when the server has received Razorpay's confirmation, which usually takes seconds.
  • If confirmation is slow, I see a message that my test will unlock shortly, a Check again button, and I can close the window safely.
  • If the payment fails I see a clear message that I was not charged, and a Try again button.
  • After success, the locked question opens straight into the tutor.
Builders: Crucial: the browser's payment success callback never unlocks anything. Only a verified Razorpay webhook (signature checked over the raw body, amount matched to the order, idempotent on event id) inserts an unlock. The price is read from the database, never from the browser.
PAY-4

Keep what I paid for

As a student, I want an unlock to cover all questions of that test permanently, so that I do not pay twice.

Acceptance criteria
  • An unlock covers every explanation and video in that test only. Other tests still follow the free plan.
  • Unlocks survive sign-out and new devices.
  • A test I already unlocked cannot be bought again.
PAY-5

See my payments

As a student, I want a Payment history screen, so that I have a record.

Acceptance criteria
  • It lists date, test, amount, status (Paid, Refunded, Failed, each with icon and text) and Razorpay payment id.
  • A paid item under review shows Refund under review.
PAY-6

Get a refund when something went wrong

As a student, I want to ask for a refund, so that I am treated fairly.

Acceptance criteria
  • The student contacts support (the contact address is configured in the app).
  • An admin flags the payment for refund with a note (see ADM-8) and issues the refund in the Razorpay dashboard.
  • When Razorpay confirms the refund, the payment shows Refunded and that test locks again automatically.

3.4 Security model (read this before changing anything)

  • Never trust the browser. Scoring, entitlement, the tutor timer, prices and unlocks are all decided on the server or in SQL.
  • Secrets live in tables students cannot read. Row-level security cannot hide single columns, which is why correct answers, explanations and tutor prompts are in separate tables (question_secrets, socratic_steps).
  • One entitlement gate. private.open_explanation decides staff preview, paid unlock, already-spent free slot, new free slot or paywall, using a lock so two parallel requests cannot exceed the free limit. Clients have no write access to unlocks, free uses, sessions or usage.
  • Payments unlock only from the webhook. HMAC-SHA256 over the raw request body, amount and currency matched to our own order, idempotent on Razorpay's event id. The browser success callback is ignored.
  • Helpers are private. Internal functions are in a private schema that the Supabase API does not expose, and execute permission is revoked from signed-in users.
  • Keys. Anything with VITE_ in its name is public. The service_role key, database URL, Anthropic key and Razorpay secrets exist only on the API host.
  • Minimal data. Chat text is never stored. Birth year only. Phone numbers are masked in admin screens. Retention job purges old rows.
  • Tutor answer-leak guard. Before showing a Claude reply, the server blocks explicit answer statements ("the answer is", option labels) and long verbatim runs from the explanation, and falls back to the scripted tutor.

3.5 Key flows

A. Take and score a test

  1. Student opens the test: browser calls start_attempt, server creates an attempt row and its clock.
  2. Student answers in the browser. Nothing is sent yet.
  3. Submit (or 00:00): browser sends {attempt, answers by question id, seconds by question id} to submit_attempt.
  4. SQL checks the attempt is the caller's and in time, compares with question_secrets, writes attempt_answers, and stores correct, wrong, skipped and score (+4 / -1 / 0).
  5. Browser reads back the attempt rows (is_correct only, never the correct option) and shows the result.

B. Open a solution and run the tutor

  1. POST /api/explain/open {attemptId, questionId}: the gate runs. Result: a session (access paid, free or staff), seconds left, first guiding question and shuffled tap choices. Or HTTP 402 paywall.
  2. Each answer: POST /api/tutor/turn {questionId, messages, kind, hintLevel}. The server loads the question and secrets, builds the prompt, calls Claude (low effort, short reply), validates the JSON, runs the leak guard, updates counters, logs token use, and returns only {verdict, reply, step, established, choices, done}. Header X-Tutor-Mode says claude, scripted or limited.
  3. Session finishes when done is true, the student taps Show me the answer (POST /api/tutor/finish skip), or the timer ends (finish timeout, checked against the server clock).
  4. GET /api/explain/:questionId returns the answer and explanation, only for a finished session. POST /api/tutor/wrapup returns the Your thinking note. POST /api/video-url returns a signed video link when allowed.

C. Pay for a test

  1. POST /api/payments/order {testId}: server reads the price from the database, creates a Razorpay order, stores a payments row (status created).
  2. Browser opens Razorpay Checkout with that order. The student pays.
  3. Razorpay calls POST /api/payments/webhook. The server verifies the signature, then SQL records the payment and inserts the unlock in one transaction.
  4. Meanwhile the browser polls GET /api/payments/status/:orderId until it says paid, then reloads entitlements.
  5. Refund: admin flags, refund is issued in Razorpay, webhook refund.processed marks Refunded and sets revoked_at on the unlock.

All routes need a signed-in user (Authorization: Bearer token) except the webhook (signature) and /health. Errors are JSON {"error": "code"}. A per-user limit of 60 requests a minute applies.

3.10 Deployment

  • Database: run supabase/setup-all.sql once in the Supabase SQL editor (or supabase db push), then seed.sql if wanted. Make the first admin with a one-line SQL update.
  • API: Render (render.yaml) or any Node 22 host using the Dockerfile. Single instance first.
  • Frontend: Vercel (vercel.json), normal build in dist/.
  • Schedule public.purge_old_data() daily (pg_cron).
  • Order: Supabase, API, frontend, then set ALLOWED_ORIGINS on the API to the frontend address, then Razorpay webhook.

3.11 Conventions

  • Colours only from the design tokens in src/styles.css; both light and dark must work.
  • Real buttons and labels, 44 pixel touch targets, visible focus. Correct and incorrect are never shown by colour alone: always an icon and text.
  • Student-facing text goes through t(); English first.
  • Admin numbers must be real. Sample or demo data is labelled as such.
  • Add a database change as a new migration file (the existing ones may be edited only until the first real deployment). Regenerate setup-all.sql with npm run gen:setup-sql.
  • The store keeps one set of action names for both the offline demo and the live backend; add new actions to both backends.

Part 4. Decisions, limits and next steps

4.1 Decisions made while building

  • The result table hides the correct option until the explanation is opened, so the tutor cannot be bypassed.
  • Questions have exactly four options. A fifth option would need a schema, scoring and tutor change.
  • Scoring is fixed at +4 / -1 / 0; a Marks field was removed from the question form.
  • Option label style is per question, not per test.
  • The guardian consent is a recorded declaration, not a verified one.
  • Hindi is a first draft; Meiteilon is intentionally empty until a translator fills it.

4.2 Known limits

  • Refreshing the page during a test loses the answers (the attempt is not resumable).
  • Video upload is a single request: a dropped connection restarts it.
  • The burst limiter is per API instance; the daily Claude limit is in the database.
  • Only the shell, sign-in and home screens are translated.
  • Nothing has been run against real third-party services yet.

4.3 Next steps

  • Go live with the checklist in docs/LAUNCH.md and run the test-mode payment, refund and video checks.
  • Legal review of the privacy policy and consent method; decide whether to verify guardians by OTP.
  • Native review of Hindi; commission Meiteilon; translate the remaining screens.
  • Resumable video upload; resumable tests after a refresh.
  • Physics and Chemistry: the schema already allows them; add content and enable the cards.
